# Getting started: a guide for people banking their voice, and the people helping them

Voice banking means recording your voice now so that, if speaking becomes hard, a communication
app can speak with a voice that sounds like you instead of a stranger's. It's often recommended
soon after an ALS/MND diagnosis, or before surgery that affects the voice box. The best time to
start is while your voice still feels like yours, but any recording is better than none.

## Before the first session

- **Pick a quiet room** you can use every time. Soft furnishings help; kitchens and bathrooms echo.
  Turn off fans, the television and music. Vocalkeep's room check tells you if it's quiet enough.
- **Use the same microphone every time.** A headset microphone keeps the distance constant, which
  helps a lot. A USB desk microphone is good too. A laptop's built-in microphone works but picks up
  more of the room.
- **Sit comfortably** with water within reach.

## A session

1. Open Vocalkeep. On **Today**, answer *How does your voice feel today?* Strong, Okay or Tired.
   Tired days get a few short sentences; that's still worth doing.
2. Read each sentence in your normal, everyday voice. It doesn't have to be perfect, and it
   shouldn't sound like a performance.
3. After each sentence Vocalkeep checks the recording and says whether to keep it or try again,
   with a tip if something could be better. **Space** records and stops, **Enter** keeps,
   **R** tries again, **L** listens back, **S** skips.
4. Vocalkeep suggests a sip of water every ten sentences and tells you when you've reached a good
   place to stop. Stop whenever you like; everything you kept is saved immediately.

Ten minutes on most days is better than an hour once a week, and much kinder to your voice.

## Your own phrases

Under **Your phrases**, add the words that are yours: family and pet names, your street, how you
answer the phone, things you always say. They're mixed into sessions, so your voice learns them,
and each recording is also kept exactly as you said it.

## Messages

Some things should be heard exactly as you say them, never as a computer voice. Under
**Messages**, record birthday wishes, advice for a wedding day, a bedtime story, "I love you".
Family members can record here too. **Save all to a folder** gives ordinary audio files and a
little web page that plays them anywhere.

## Keeping your recordings safe

Your voice bank is irreplaceable. Use **Settings › Voice bank › Back up** after each week of
recording and keep the zip somewhere else: a USB stick, another computer, a cloud drive you trust.

## Building the voice

When **Progress** reaches *Natural* (about 600 sentences), go to **My voice**. Building needs a
computer with a graphics card; it can be someone else's. See [training.md](training.md), then
[using-your-voice.md](using-your-voice.md) to put the voice into Saywell, an Android phone,
Windows or Linux.

## For clinicians

Vocalkeep is free and runs entirely offline, so it can be used in clinics without data-sharing
agreements. The voice bank is a plain folder ([format](voice-bank-format.md)); recordings are
16-bit WAV at the microphone's native rate, so they can also be used with other voice banking
services. Vocalkeep is not a medical device.
